The beat you described: 草 means LOL in Japanese, but the same character is a
vulgarity in Chinese. Two things you may want to work in, both factual, both
yours to confirm:

- Where the Japanese meaning comes from — 笑 (warai) → w → wwww → the row of
  w's looks like grass → 草. That origin is what makes it land.
- The tone distinction. 草 is cǎo (third tone, "grass"); the word it stands in
  for is cào (fourth tone). The Chinese reading is a deliberate near-miss used
  to soften the real word, which is why it reads as a swear and not as a noun.
